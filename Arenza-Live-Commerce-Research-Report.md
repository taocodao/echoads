Arenza sits at the intersection of five converging markets — sports fan engagement, video game mechanics, live auctions, shoppable streaming, and interactive second-screen TV — each independently worth tens of billions of dollars. ## Part I: The Live Commerce Market

### 1.1 What Is Live Commerce?

Live commerce is the convergence of live-streaming entertainment with real-time, frictionless purchasing. Unlike static ecommerce (browse → click → checkout) or traditional TV shopping (passively watch → call a number), live commerce is *participatory*: viewers interact via polls, comments, questions, and instant checkout buttons — all while the stream is playing[cite:475]. The format compresses the traditional purchase funnel from days to minutes, and in some models, seconds.

The purchase psychology is distinct. Three forces drive conversion in live commerce that are absent from standard ecommerce:

1. **Scarcity signals** — "Only 12 left at this price" visible in real time
2. **Social proof** — visible purchase count, live chat reactions, co-viewers
3. **FOMO urgency** — the stream ends, the deal disappears

Together, these create what researchers describe as a *hedonic shopping experience*: the act of watching *is* entertaining independently of whether a purchase occurs[cite:469]. This mirrors exactly the dynamic Arenza creates through sports bingo, predictions, and trivia — fans are already in a heightened emotional state and already have their phone in hand.

### 1.3 The Second-Screen Opportunity

Nearly **two-thirds of US social network users** are forecast to actively use a second screen (smartphone or tablet) while watching TV or streaming in 2026[cite:467]. Among sports fans specifically, Gen Z viewers are **21% more likely than average** to be gaming on a mobile device while watching sports[cite:60]. Critically for Arenza, **65% of second-screeners** use their device to look up products or visit advertiser sites while streaming[cite:465].

Mobile devices account for **55.2% of all live streaming** in 2024, confirming the smartphone as the dominant second-screen device[cite:458]. The purchase journey has compressed: awareness, consideration, and conversion can now happen in minutes on the same device[cite:467].

Among fans who engage digitally during games, **75% say they spend more money at the venue** when the digital fan experience is strong, and digitally engaged fans are **44% more likely to purchase future tickets**[cite:216]. Arenza's attached market research confirms these behavior patterns map directly to its audience.

## Part II: Leading Live Commerce Platforms

### 2.1 Taobao Live (Alibaba) — The Originator

**What it is:** Launched by Alibaba in 2016, Taobao Live is the world's largest live commerce platform. In 2020 alone, it generated over **$61 billion USD in GMV**[cite:472]. It invented the core live commerce mechanic that all subsequent platforms emulate.

**How it achieves results:**
- **Host-led discovery:** KOLs (Key Opinion Leaders) and nano-influencers demonstrate products live, answer real-time questions, and trigger countdown deals
- **One-tap buy:** A persistent shopping cart overlay allows viewers to tap any product shown and instantly add to cart without leaving the stream
- **Flash coupons:** Time-limited coupon codes shown exclusively during the live session drive 40–60% of all purchases
- **Algorithmic amplification:** Taobao's algorithm surfaces streams where purchase velocity is highest, creating a virality feedback loop
- **Technology stack:** RTMP/HLS streaming protocols, AI-powered product recognition from the live feed, real-time inventory synchronization, and sub-200ms latency checkout[cite:478]

**Key lesson for Arenza:** The coupon-drop mechanic during live moments is Taobao Live's most transferable insight. Coupons issued *only* during specific in-game events (a BINGO, a correct prediction) create exactly the scarcity and exclusivity that drive 40–50% lower return rates[cite:458].

### 2.2 TikTok Shop — The Social Commerce Disruptor

**What it is:** TikTok Shop launched in the US in September 2023 and grew US monthly GMV from **$15.1 million (July 2023) to $1.1 billion (July 2025)** — a 73x increase in 24 months[cite:459]. TikTok Shop is now the most important live commerce platform in the Western world.

**How it achieves results:**
- **Frictionless native checkout:** The purchase button overlays the live video without navigation away — the stream continues playing while checkout completes
- **Creator-affiliate model:** Over 74,000 creators have featured QVC products alone in shoppable TikTok videos[cite:460], creating a distributed sales force
- **"Super LIVE" broadcasts:** Scheduled high-production streams with celebrity hosts drive record single-day GMV events
- **72% daily live sessions growth:** TikTok reported a 72% increase in daily live shopping sessions heading into Black Friday 2025[cite:463]
- **8 million hours:** In 2024 alone, brands and creators hosted over 8 million hours of live shopping on TikTok Shop in the US[cite:463]
- **76% conversion:** A TikTok survey found that 76% of consumers who engaged with TikTok Shop bought something from a livestream in the past year[cite:463]

**Key lesson for Arenza:** The "don't leave the stream" principle is paramount. Every purchase interaction should feel native to the viewing experience. The coupon-to-cart flow must be completable in under 30 seconds without the fan losing their game context.

### 2.3 QVC — The Legacy Innovator

**What it is:** QVC, self-described as "the original innovator in live shopping," produces over **40,000 hours of live shoppable content per year** and ships **200 million+ units annually**[cite:460]. In April 2025, it launched 24/7 live shopping streams on TikTok, bridging linear and digital[cite:462].

**How it achieves results:**
- **Always-on content calendar:** The 24/7 model removes the "when is the next stream?" friction — there is always something live
- **Host personality as trust signal:** Consistent hosts who build parasocial relationships dramatically increase lifetime purchase frequency
- **Scarcity narration:** QVC hosts verbally count down remaining inventory ("We're down to 47 of these"), a technique that increases purchase urgency measurably

**Key lesson for Arenza:** The always-on approach suggests Arenza should have non-game-day content — daily trivia, weekly challenges, evergreen earn mechanics — so the shop experience stays alive between sports events.

### 2.4 TSN / The CW Network — Sports Shoppable Broadcasting

**What it is:** Canadian sports network TSN launched shoppable broadcasts for CFL fans through a partnership with Shopsense AI, allowing fans to scan an on-screen QR code and access a virtual store with team gear *during live Friday Night Football games*[cite:463]. The CW Network simultaneously announced a partnership with Shopsense for ACC college football, allowing purchase of game-day essentials directly from mobile during live broadcasts[cite:464].

**How it achieves results:**
- **QR code activation:** Low-friction entry — no app download required. Fans scan, browse, and buy in under 60 seconds
- **AI-curated collections:** Shopsense AI surfaces products contextually relevant to what's happening in the broadcast — a touchdown triggers jersey drops, a halftime show surfaces branded merchandise[cite:464]
- **Revenue sharing:** Media companies monetize their content through affiliate commissions on every sale triggered by the broadcast — a fundamentally new revenue stream for sports broadcasters[cite:464]

**Key lesson for Arenza:** The AI-contextual commerce layer — where the shop automatically populates with items relevant to the live game moment — is the next evolution Arenza should build toward. When the Chiefs score, Chiefs gear surfaces at the top of the shop.

### 2.5 Amazon Live & Shoppable TV

**What it is:** Amazon has taken a distinct approach: focusing on **shoppable TV** to reach shoppers during content they are already consuming[cite:459]. Unlike TikTok's creator-led model, Amazon Live integrates product purchase into its Prime Video content, with carousels of shoppable products visible alongside the video.

**Key lesson for Arenza:** Amazon demonstrates that large media companies treat shoppable streaming as a loyalty play, not just a sales channel. Every purchase deepens the Prime relationship. Arenza's point-earn-shop loop creates the same stickiness.

## Part III: Live Commerce Design Principles

### 3.1 The Core UX Architecture

The most successful live commerce platforms share five non-negotiable design principles:

**1. Zero-navigation checkout**
The product card, add-to-cart, and payment confirmation must all be completable without leaving the primary content experience. Every additional screen or navigation step costs approximately 20% of conversion intent[cite:461].

**2. Persistent points/balance visibility**
Users must always know their purchasing power. The balance display in the header (Arenza's implementation) is the correct pattern — users who can see they can afford an item convert at 3x the rate of users who must navigate to check[cite:477].

**3. Time-pressure mechanics**
Countdown timers, flash coupon expiry, and "X left" inventory signals are the most effective single design elements in live commerce. They must be visually prominent (large font, accent color) and real (countdown must update in real time)[cite:458][cite:474].

**4. Coupon system design**
The Arenza coupon system must fulfill three functions simultaneously:
- **Acquisition:** Sponsor-branded coupons drive partner ROI (Pepsi, Nike, DoorDash)
- **Retention:** Reward coupons issued post-purchase (REWARD + code) bring users back
- **Attribution:** Every coupon code encodes its source event (BINGO, prediction win, trivia), creating a complete data trail from fan action to sponsor redemption[cite:474]

**5. Mobile-first, thumb-zone priority**
55.2% of live streaming happens on mobile[cite:458]. The primary action button (Add to Cart, Pay with Points) must be in the bottom 40% of the screen — within thumb reach. Filter controls, sort, and navigation should be compact and collapsible on mobile.

### 3.2 The Points Economy Design

The attached Arenza product brief establishes a points-earn-shop-coupon loop. Live commerce research confirms this architecture is structurally sound. Key calibration principles:

| Mechanic | Suggested Points | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Sports Bingo (per BINGO) | 250 pts | High engagement, 5–8 min per round |
| Correct Prediction | 150–200 pts | Moderate difficulty, drives watch time |
| Live Trivia (correct answer) | 100 pts | Low friction, high frequency |
| Watch Bonus (per quarter) | 50 pts | Rewards passive viewing |
| Social Share | 75 pts | Extends reach organically |
| First Purchase | 500 pts | Acquisition incentive |

The **median item value should target 600–1,200 points** — achievable in a single game session. Items priced above 3,000 points serve as aspirational "stretch goals" that drive repeat engagement across multiple games[cite:474][cite:480].

### 3.3 The Coupon Wallet Architecture

Based on research across Taobao Live, SQWAD, and Arenza's own product brief, the coupon system should operate on three tiers:

**Tier 1 — Game Coupons (Earned in-game)**
- Issued instantly upon triggering event (BINGO, prediction win)
- Brand-specific (PEPSI10, NIKE15) — partner-funded, zero cost to Arenza
- Short expiry (7–14 days) to drive urgency
- Encoded source: every code contains the mechanic that generated it

**Tier 2 — Purchase Reward Coupons (Post-checkout)**
- Issued automatically on order confirmation (e.g., REWARD4521)
- Fixed discount (200 pts) to incentivize immediate return visit
- Longer expiry (30 days) — this is the retention mechanic
- Acts as Arenza's primary repeat-purchase driver

**Tier 3 — Sponsor Drop Coupons (Live event exclusive)**
- Available only during specified game moments
- Highest value (WIN500 class) to maximize perceived value
- QR code or push notification delivery to maximize reach
- Full attribution chain to sponsor — activations are reportable

### 3.4 The Product Catalog Architecture

The prize shop should be structured across five categories that directly map to sponsor verticals:

| Category | Examples | Sponsor Type | Avg. Points Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Apparel** | Jerseys, caps, hoodies | Nike, Fanatics, team stores | 800–3,500 pts |
| **Gift Cards** | Amazon, Best Buy, iTunes | Retail, tech sponsors | 400–1,500 pts |
| **Food & Drink** | DoorDash credits, Pepsi bundles | QSR, beverage sponsors | 350–700 pts |
| **Experiences** | Stadium passes, meet & greets | Team, venue, Arenza premium | 3,000–7,500 pts |
| **Sponsor Deals** | Branded bundles, exclusive offers | Cross-category sponsors | 250–1,200 pts |

**Affordability signaling** is critical: items priced within the user's current balance should be visually distinguished (gold color, "Redeem Now" label) from near-reach items (neutral, "Almost there") and out-of-reach items (muted, "Earn more"). This drives both immediate conversion and aspirational engagement simultaneously[cite:477].

## Part IV: Arenza Ecommerce Site — Design Specification

### 4.1 Art Direction

**Concept:** Sports Energy × Clean Commerce  
**Tone:** High-energy but focused. Dark surfaces with electric orange-gold accents. The aesthetic of a modern sports arena combined with the clarity of a premium ecommerce store.

**Typography:**
- **Display (headings, prices, CTAs):** Space Grotesk — geometric, confident, readable at small sizes
- **Body (labels, descriptions, meta):** Inter — the gold standard for UI clarity

**Color System:**
- Primary surface: `#0a0c10` (near-black, sports arena darkness)
- Accent: `#ff5c00` (electric orange — energy, urgency)
- Gold: `#f0a500` (points currency — distinct from action orange)
- Success: `#22c55e` (affordable, earned)
- Dividers/borders: `rgba(255,255,255,0.08)` (alpha-blended, dark-mode correct)

### 4.2 Site Architecture — Four Pages

**Page 1: Prize Shop (Home)**
The primary ecommerce experience. Contains:
- Live game banner (sticky, shows score + countdown — always visible)
- Hero section with personal stats (your points, item count, coupon count)
- Earn Points Quick Games panel (three game cards — tap to earn points immediately)
- Halftime Drop Alert with live countdown timer
- Category filter bar (All / Apparel / Gift Cards / Experiences / Food / Sponsor Deals) + sort dropdown
- Product grid with affordability color-coding and one-tap Add to Cart

**Page 2: Cart & Checkout with Coupon Tracker**
- Cart items list with emoji product imagery, brand, name, points price, and remove button
- Order Summary panel (sticky on desktop) containing:
  - Points balance bar with visual progress indicator
  - Applied coupon line items (green, with discount shown)
  - Coupon input field with auto-uppercase formatting
  - Saved Coupons (tap-to-apply buttons, grayed-out when used)
  - Order total with real-time discount calculation
  - "Pay with Points" CTA (disabled if insufficient balance)
- Post-checkout confirmation with new reward coupon code display + confetti animation

**Page 3: Live Drop**
- Simulated broadcast window with team score, viewer count, live badge
- In-Game Activities panel (Bingo / Prediction / Trivia — each tap-to-earn)
- Live Drop sidebar with featured item (timer countdown), remaining drops
- "Claim Now" CTA that adds to cart and navigates to checkout

**Page 4: Coupon Wallet**
- Grid of all coupons (earned, sponsor-issued, reward)
- Each coupon shows: brand, discount value, description, source event, expiry date, status badge (Active / Used / Expired)
- Active coupons have full-color gradient header; inactive coupons are muted
- Coupon code displayed in monospace for easy copy

### 4.3 Key Interaction Patterns

**Points Earned Popup:** When a user taps any game card and earns points, a centered modal with gold border, bouncy scale animation, and "+250 pts" display fires for 2.2 seconds — then auto-dismisses. No close button required — this is a celebration, not a dialog.

**Toast Notifications:** All secondary feedback (Add to Cart, Coupon Applied, Error) fires as a bottom-right toast with icon, bold title, and contextual body — auto-dismissing after 3.5 seconds.

**Confetti Animation:** On successful checkout, 70 colored particles rain from the top of the viewport. The new reward coupon code is displayed prominently in a dashed gold box.

**Real-time Timers:** The Halftime Drop countdown, banner game clock, and Live Drop item countdown all run simultaneously via JavaScript intervals — creating a persistent sense of live urgency even when no broadcast is present.

**Coupon Auto-Apply:** Saved coupons in the Cart panel are rendered as clickable tags. Tapping any tag auto-fills the coupon input and triggers the apply logic — reducing entry friction to a single tap.

## Part V: B2B Monetization & Sponsor Integration

### 5.1 The Sponsor Value Proposition

Live commerce research consistently confirms that branded coupons issued through interactive games achieve **3.2× higher redemption rates** than standard digital advertising[cite:177]. For Arenza's B2B partners, the product offers:

**Measurable attribution:** Every coupon contains a source event code (PEPSI10 = Pepsi × Halftime Trivia). The platform can report: impressions → engagements → coupon issues → coupon redemptions → purchase conversions — a complete funnel with dollar-amount ROI at every step.

**Premium placement:** Sponsor deals category in the prize shop; sponsor branding on reward coupons issued post-purchase; "sponsored by" labels on specific BINGO cells or prediction prompts.

**First-party data:** Every coupon issuance and redemption event captures fan identity, behavioral segment (apparel fan vs. food fan), game engagement depth, and purchase intent — data that Facebook and Google audiences cannot provide.

### 5.2 Sponsor Tier Structure

| Tier | Annual Investment | Deliverables |
|---|---|---|
| **Presenting Sponsor** | $250K+ | Branded bingo board, custom coupon series, live drop naming rights, full data package |
| **Category Sponsor** | $75–150K | One product category ownership, 3 coupon drops per season, monthly report |
| **Activation Sponsor** | $25–50K | Single-game coupon activation, branded prize items, performance dashboard |
| **Starter** | $5–15K | One coupon code per event, prize shop listing, basic impression data |

### 5.3 The Live Commerce Revenue Model for Arenza

| Revenue Stream | Mechanism | Unit Economics |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor coupon activation | Fee per coupon series issued | $5K–25K per series |
| Prize shop GMV commission | % of points-funded purchases | 15–25% margin |
| Data licensing | Behavioral segment reports | $10K–50K per partner per season |
| White-label platform | License to other sports networks | $100K–500K/year SaaS |
| Media placement | In-stream banner / video integration | CPM-based, $15–40 CPM |

## Part VI: Key Takeaways & Strategic Recommendations

### What the Market Teaches

1. **The stream must never stop for a purchase.** Every millisecond of checkout friction loses fans. The points-pay model (no card entry, no address form) is the single biggest conversion advantage Arenza has over traditional ecommerce[cite:461].

2. **Coupons are the glue between engagement and commerce.** Taobao Live, TikTok Shop, and SQWAD all prove that exclusive, time-limited coupons issued during specific event moments are the highest-converting commercial mechanic in live commerce[cite:458][cite:474].

3. **51% of NFL fans already shop during games.** The Arenza audience is pre-conditioned to purchase during live sports. The platform is not creating a new behavior — it is providing a structured, rewarded channel for a behavior that already exists organically[cite:470].

4. **Second-screen is now first-screen for engagement.** With two-thirds of US viewers using a second screen in 2026, the smartphone during a live game is not a distraction from the sport — it is an extension of the fan experience[cite:467].

5. **Free-to-play removes all regulatory barriers.** Unlike PrizePicks (DFS, state licensing required) or live betting (heavy regulation), Arenza's points-earn-redeem model operates in the same legal space as a loyalty program — deployable in all 50 US states with no gaming licenses[cite:175].

6. **The data is the product.** TSN, The CW, and Amazon Live all treat shoppable content as a data acquisition strategy as much as a revenue channel. Arenza should position its first-party fan behavioral data as a core B2B deliverable alongside coupon ROI metrics[cite:464].

7. **Context-driven commerce converts best.** Shopsense AI's model — where product suggestions respond to live broadcast events — is the next evolution. A Chiefs touchdown should surface Chiefs jerseys. A halftime show should trigger food delivery credits. Arenza's roadmap should include this AI-contextual layer[cite:463][cite:464].
